About Me

I'm a Computer Science Engineering student at The National Institute of Engineering, Mysuru, graduating in 2027. I care less about using the trendiest tool and more about understanding why a system behaves the way it does — which is why most of my projects end up living at the edge: on-device AI inference, low-latency retrieval, systems that work even when the cloud isn't available.

My approach to problem-solving is architecture-first. Before I write a single line of implementation code, I ask: what are the constraints, what breaks first at scale, and what's the simplest design that survives contact with reality. I'd rather ship a system with 4 well-reasoned components than 10 loosely connected ones.

I also believe engineering is a team sport even when you're the only one coding — I document design decisions as I make them, not after, because the “why” behind a choice is often more valuable than the choice itself.

When I'm not building, I'm usually deep in a rabbit hole about distributed systems, quantum computing, or arguing with myself about whether space-based data centers are 10 years away or 30.

AI Ghost — On-Device Memory Resurrection System

2025–2026
Android · Python · FastAPI · AWS S3 · Gemma 3 (1B INT4) · MediaPipe · sentence-transformers
github.com/kadv19/AiGhost ↗

A privacy-first Android system that lets you conversationally query your old phone's data from your new device — with zero cloud AI involved.

I led a 3-person team through the full build: a Python backend generates 384-dimensional embeddings via sentence-transformers, an on-device RAG pipeline performs cosine similarity retrieval in under 50ms, and Gemma 3 1B (INT4 quantized) runs fully offline inference through MediaPipe's LLM Inference API.

The part I'm proudest of is the authentication design: instead of building an auth server, I baked a 4-word passphrase directly into the S3 path structure — security through architecture, not infrastructure. No database, no session management, no attack surface to maintain.

Deepfake Detection via Physiological Signal Analysis

2025
Python · FastAPI · OpenCV · SciPy · Scikit-learn

A detection system that catches deepfakes using something most detectors ignore: your heartbeat. I implemented Eulerian Video Magnification to amplify photoplethysmography (PPG) signals — subtle, invisible skin-color shifts correlated with blood flow — that are present in real footage but usually absent in synthetic video.

Achieved 85% detection accuracy across 150+ videos from the FaceForensics++ dataset, with the full pipeline exposed via a FastAPI service returning confidence scores in under 30 seconds per video. Added result caching that cut disk I/O by 60%.

The interesting part wasn't the ML — it was realizing that physiological signals are a much harder thing for generative models to fake convincingly than pixel-level artifacts.
