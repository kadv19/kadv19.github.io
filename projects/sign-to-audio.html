Sign-to-Audio Wearable Translation System

Deep Learning · NLP · Computer Vision · Python

In Progress — 85%

Overview

Camera-embedded wearable glasses that translate sign language into spoken audio in real time.

Problem

Real-time sign language translation for accessibility exists in research but rarely as a genuinely wearable, low-latency, real-world product. Most solutions require a phone held up and pointed — not a natural, hands-free experience.

Architecture

Camera capture → CNN gesture classifier → NLP sentence reconstruction → speech synthesis, as three independent, swappable components connected by an async pipeline.

Technical Deep-Dive

CNN-based hand-sign classifier currently at 85% recognition accuracy. NLP layer reconstructs grammatically correct sentences from discrete, individually-recognized gesture tokens (gesture recognition alone gives you words, not sentences). Components are deliberately decoupled — gesture recognition doesn't wait on speech synthesis, enabling independent scaling and testing. Actively optimizing for edge deployment: model quantization to fit wearable hardware constraints, async processing to minimize perceived latency.

Challenges

Reconstructing natural sentence structure from a bag of individually-classified gestures (order and grammar aren't 1:1 with sign order). Fitting inference on genuinely low-power wearable hardware, not a phone.

My Role

Solo project, ongoing — architecture, CNN training, NLP integration, edge optimization.

Results

85% gesture recognition accuracy; end-to-end pipeline functional, edge optimization ongoing.
